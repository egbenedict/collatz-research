# Reading notes: Tao, "Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values"

Source: [arXiv:1909.03562](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562), read in **v7 (16 Jul 2026)**, 58 pp.
Published in *Forum Math. Pi* 10 (2022) e12. Local copy: `../pdfs/tao2019/` (PDF + extracted text).
Read 2026-11-21.

## 1. What it proves, and what it does not

**Theorem 1.3.** If f(N) → ∞, then Col_min(N) < f(N) for almost all N, in *logarithmic*
density.

**Theorem 3.1 (quantitative form).** For N₀ ≥ 2 and every x,

    P( Syr_min(Log(odd ∩ [1,x])) > N₀ ) ≪ (log N₀)^(−c).

Here Log(·) samples N with weight 1/N, and the constant c > 0 is not made explicit. In words:
apart from a log-density fraction of about (log N₀)^(−c), every orbit drops below N₀.

What it does **not** say:
- Nothing about individual orbits. A single divergent orbit, or a single cycle, is invisible:
  both have density 0.
- It does not say that almost all orbits reach 1. Remark 1.4 argues that "Col_min ≤ C₀ for
  almost all N" with a fixed C₀ is about as hard as the full conjecture. This is now
  rigorous, given the 2026 predecessor-density claims (§5).

## 2. The proof, as a chain of reductions

Each step reduces to the next one:

| Step | Statement | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Thm 1.3 ⇐ Thm 1.6 | Collatz ↔ Syracuse map Syr(N) = (3N+1)/2^ν₂(3N+1) on odd N | Exactly one ×3 per step, so 3-adic analysis is natural |
| Thm 1.6 ⇐ Prop 1.11 | **Stabilisation of first passage.** N_y ~ Log on [y, y^α] with α = 1.001. Then Pass_x(N_{x^α}) and Pass_x(N_{x^{α²}}) are within log^(−c) x in total variation | A Bourgain-style "approximately invariant family of measures" replaces an invariant measure, which can't exist here. Iterate x → x^{1/α} down to N₀ and telescope (§3) |
| Prop 1.11 ⇐ Props 1.9 + 1.14 | Step back m₀ ≈ c·log x steps from the passage time | Prop 1.9: for n ≲ log N, the valuations (a₁…aₙ) are ≈ iid Geom(2) (2-adic equidistribution). After stepping back, Pass_x depends on y only through Syr^n(N) mod 3^(n−m₀) = F(ā) mod 3^(n−m₀) |
| Prop 1.14 ⇐ Prop 1.17 | **Fine-scale mixing**: Syrac(Z/3ⁿ) is close to uniform on cosets of 3^m Z/3ⁿ | Split Xₙ at a stopping time k ≈ n·log3/(2log2). The head F_{k+1} is **injective** mod 3ⁿ (Lemma 6.2, Cor 6.3: an elementary 2-adic valuation argument), so it has large collision entropy. The tail is independent with small Fourier coefficients. Then apply Plancherel |
| Prop 1.17 ⇐ Prop 7.3 | **Characteristic-function decay**: E e(ξ·Syrac(Z/3ⁿ)/3ⁿ) ≪_A n^(−A) for 3 ∤ ξ, uniformly in ξ | Pair the terms: b_j = a_{2j−1} + a_{2j} ~ Pascal. Conditional on b_j = 3 (probability 1/4) the pair is (1,2) or (2,1), giving the factor \|cos(π θ(j, b[1,j]))\|. Call a lattice point "white" if \|θ\| > ε; each white visit gains exp(−ε³) |
| Prop 7.3 ⇐ Lemma 7.4 + Prop 7.8 | A 2-D renewal process with increments Hold (mean (4,16)) visits ≫ log n white points | **Black points form disjoint "triangles"** (slope log9/log2 ≈ 3.17), separated by ≥ (1/10)·log(1/ε). The walk has slope 4 > 3.17, so it exits through the top edge and then hits white. A downward induction on m = ⌊n/2⌋ − j has three cases: white point, small triangle, large triangle. Large triangles are rarely met right after a long crossing (Lemma 7.10) |

Where the arithmetic of 3 and 2 enters (Conway test): only in Lemma 6.2 / Cor 6.3
(injectivity of offsets) and Lemma 7.4 (θ(j+1, l) = 9θ, θ(j, l−1) = 2θ, and the fact that
ξ·3^(n−1)·(stuff)/3ⁿ ∈ {1/3, 2/3}). Baker's theorem is mentioned (Remark 7.5) but not used.

Errata: in July 2026 Lech Mazur reported a gap in the v4/v5 §6 buffer (C_A² log n). Tao's fix
was to change the constant 1/2 to 0.99 in (6.7) onward. v7 contains the fix: see (6.7) and
the "0.4175 < 0.99·log 2 ≈ 0.6862" check in Cor 6.3.

## 3. Where the losses are

1. **Logarithmic, not natural, density.** The cause is the exp(O(√n)) multiplicative
   fluctuation in Syr^n(N) ≈ (3/4)ⁿ N (1.17). Natural density would need mixing of the whole
   random affine map, not just its offset (Remark 1.16). Claimed done in 2026 (§5).
2. **Superpolynomial, not exponential, decay** in Prop 1.17. Tao: "perhaps" exp(−cn) is
   true. A random-map heuristic (4ⁿ inputs to 3ⁿ outputs) predicts exp(−cm) in (1.26).
3. **log^(−c) x error in (1.20).** This is the source of the (log N₀)^(−c) rate in Thm 3.1.
   The telescoping over scales x_j = y^(α^j) sums Σ(α^j log y)^(−c) ≈ (log N₀)^(−c)/(1−α^(−c)).
   With α = 1.001 that factor is about 1000/c. Tao says x^(−c) might be possible. That would
   make the Thm 3.1 rate polynomial in N₀.
4. **Constants are not explicit.** Because of the ε³ gains and A-dependent constants C_A,
   C_{A,ε} and P, any explicit version has astronomically bad constants (see the 2026 numbers
   in §5).

## 4. Kill tests: why this line cannot reach "every N" on its own

- **5x+1:** passes. The method needs negative drift log 3 − 2 log 2 < 0; for 5x+1 the drift
  log 5 − 2 log 2 > 0.
- **Negative integers / 3x−1: this is the decisive limitation.** On positive integers, 3x−1
  has Syracuse map Aff_a(x) = (3x−1)/2^a. Its offsets are −Fₙ(ā), so its Syracuse random
  variable is the negation of ours. Its characteristic function is the complex conjugate,
  with exactly the same bounds, and Props 1.9, 1.11 and 1.14 go through verbatim. So **Tao's
  theorem holds for 3x−1 as well**, and 3x−1 has three cycles (1; 5,7,10; 17,…). Any extension
  that uses only Tao's estimates can therefore prove "almost all orbits enter a bounded set",
  never "reach 1". The only 3x+1-specific input in the 2026 follow-ups is **finite
  verification** (orbits below 2^71 reach 1).
- **The predecessor obstruction.** The 2026 claims (§5) give positive lower density d_y for
  the set of n reaching y, for each y with 3 ∤ y. But d_y has no positive lower bound
  uniform in y (disjoint basins). A counterexample orbit would live above 2^71, so its basin
  density could be arbitrarily small. Hence:
  **Collatz ⟺ the set of n reaching 1 has natural density exactly 1**, while
  "density ≥ 1 − ε" for any fixed ε > 0 proves nothing about individual orbits. Probabilistic
  error terms never become exactly 0. This is the precise sense in which the method is
  "one step short", and the missing step is the whole conjecture.

## 6. What is genuinely left in this line

Ranked by value to us. None of these proves Collatz (§4).

1. **Good constants (open, and meaningful).** Prove something like "at least half of all
   integers reach 1". The current constants are 2^(−2^(2^163)), while numerics say the true
   answer is 1. The route: polynomial error in (1.20) (loss 3), so Thm 3.1 becomes
   1 − C·N₀^(−c), then plug in N₀ = 2^71. The bottleneck is explicit, decent constants in the
   Fourier decay (Prop 1.17) and the mixing step.
2. **Exponential Fourier decay (Tao's question).** Prove |E e(ξ·Syrac(Z/3ⁿ)/3ⁿ)| ≤ C·ρⁿ for
   some ρ < 1, uniformly over ξ with 3 ∤ ξ. It feeds item 1. **A cheap computation first:**
   compute max_ξ |S_χ(n)| exactly for n up to about 15. Use the backward transfer operator
   h_i(e) = Σ_a 2^(−a) χ(3^(i−1)·2^(−(e+a)))·h_{i+1}(e+a) on the cyclic group
   (Z/3^k)^× = ⟨2⟩, where the sum over a is a convolution (FFT, O(M log M) with
   M = 2·3^(n−1)). This shows the true decay rate, and the extremal ξ hint at the worst
   "triangle" geometry. [arXiv:1608.03600](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03600) reportedly finds
   that every nontrivial eigenvalue of the mod-3^k residue chain has modulus 1/2, which
   suggests ρ = 1/2 or something like it.
   **Done (2026-11-22, I-007):** the decay is e^(−I₀n)·n^(−c), with I₀ = I(log₂3) = 0.05498
   and c ≈ 1. The extremal ξ = 2^k has k ≈ ⌊n·log₂3⌋ − 7. There is no ρ = 1/2. The n ≤ 17 data
   look like exp(−c√n), but that is a transient. This matches the WakauraH conjecture
   Θ(2^(−I₀m)·m^(−c)).
3. **Verify a 2026 claim.** For example, build the Mazur or Lau Lean projects and audit their
   statements, as we did in I-005. That is useful hygiene, but it doesn't move the frontier.
